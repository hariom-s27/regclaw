# Obligation labelling guideline v1.1 (2026-10-18)

v1.0 (sections A–H) is unchanged. v1.1 **adds** the rules below, found in the round-1 adjudication. They apply to all future labelling (the retest round, round 2). Round 1 is kept as it was, with adjudicated finals.

**G1. Imperatives.** A command with no modal ("Use equation A-2…", "Keep a written record…", "Add a description…") is **SHALL**.

**G2. Partial definition / scope.** Rule B1 (definition / scope / heading → NONE) applies only when the **whole** clause is a definition, scope, purpose or heading. If the clause also contains a separate operative rule (shall / must / should / may), label that rule, strongest wins. Examples: rows 122, 132.

**G3. Quotations.** A sentence quoted from another document (e.g. an ISO "shall" quoted inside FDA guidance) doesn't change the label. Label what *this* document says.

**G4. Deeming provisions.** "X shall be considered as Y" / "shall be deemed" assigns a legal status, not a duty → **NONE**, unless the clause also has an operative rule.

**G5. Forms and templates.** A blank form, receipt or memorandum → **NONE**, unless it contains a signed undertaking with a duty ("I shall comply…", "I undertake to comply…") → **SHALL**, low_confidence=1.

**G6. "may" = possibility vs permission.** "may arise / may lead to / may be needed / may not be necessary" describe possibility → ignore. "may submit / may use / may choose / the authority may cancel" give permission → MAY. "may … only if…" restricts → SHALL.

**G7. Restated law with "may not".** "FDA may not withhold clearance…" (restating a statute) = prohibition → SHALL, restated_reg=1.
